Classic Artisan Sourdough

The Importance of an Active Starter

The foundation of any great sourdough loaf lies in the vitality of its wild yeast culture. Before you even think about mixing your dough, ensure your starter is bubbly, active, and has at least doubled in size after its last feeding. A mature starter provides the essential leavening power and complex flavor profile that commercial yeast simply cannot replicate.

Mastering the Stretch and Fold Technique

Unlike traditional yeasted breads that require intensive kneading, artisan sourdough benefits from gentle development. The 'stretch and fold' method aligns the gluten network without aggressively degassing the dough. Over a bulk fermentation period of four to six hours, performing this technique every thirty minutes will transform a shaggy mass into a smooth, elastic dough capable of holding its shape during baking.

Scoring and Baking with Steam

Scoring the top of your loaf before baking isn't just for aesthetics; it provides a controlled weak point for the dough to expand rapidly (oven spring) without tearing randomly. To achieve that signature blistered and crackling crust, moisture is crucial in the initial baking phase. Using a pre-heated cast-iron Dutch oven traps the steam escaping from the dough, mimicking a professional bakery deck oven perfectly.
